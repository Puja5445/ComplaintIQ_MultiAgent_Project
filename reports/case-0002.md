# ComplaintIQ report case-0002

# A. INTERNAL CASE NOTE

**Case Type:** Alleged Unauthorized EFT — ATM Withdrawals
**Severity:** CRITICAL — Vulnerable Customer Flag (customer self-reports advanced age; claim reported as unresolved for an extended period)
**Product:** Checking/Savings Account
**Regulatory Framework:** EFTA / Regulation E [C:exam_manual-p677-c0]

**Summary of Allegation (per customer's own statement — unverified):**
Customer states she is 72 years old and that two $500 ATM withdrawals on 3/3 were made without her authorization. Customer states she notified the bank the same day. Customer states approximately three weeks have passed without a refund or resolution communicated to her. These figures and dates come solely from the customer's account and have not been independently verified against bank records. These are allegations only; no liability, fraud determination, or admission of bank error has been made.

**Regulatory Considerations:**
- ATM withdrawals fall within the definition of "electronic fund transfer" under Reg E [C:exam_manual-p680-c0].
- An "unauthorized EFT" is a transfer initiated by someone other than the consumer, without authority, from which the consumer receives no benefit. This definition excludes transfers by a person the consumer furnished the access device to (unless the consumer later notified the institution that person's access was revoked), transfers involving fraudulent intent by the consumer or someone acting in concert with the consumer, and transfers made by the institution or its employee [C:exam_manual-p682-c0]. Fact-finding should confirm whether any shared access device or PIN disclosure applies before this is treated as unauthorized.
- Consumer liability for unauthorized transfers depends on timely notice. If the consumer notifies the institution within two business days of learning of the loss/theft of the access device, liability is limited to the lesser of $50 or the amount of unauthorized transfers occurring before notice [C:12CFR1005.6-c0]. The evidence available does not address the liability tier applicable when notice is given after two business days; this should be confirmed against the full text of 12 CFR 1005.6(b) before any liability determination is communicated.
- Notice may be given in person, by phone, or in writing, and is effective even if the consumer cannot provide the exact account/card number, so long as the account is reasonably identifiable [C:exam_manual-p699-c0]. The date, method, and content of the customer's alleged same-day report must be verified against bank records (call logs, branch notes, written notice) before timeliness is confirmed.
- If a consumer's own delay in notifying the institution of a lost/stolen access device was due to extenuating circumstances (e.g., travel, hospitalization), the notification window is extended to a reasonable time [C:exam_manual-p699-c0]. This provision addresses delay on the consumer's side in reporting, not the institution's timeline for investigating or responding to a claim already reported.

2. Confirm current internal investigation status and any correspondence already sent to the customer; do not reference provisional credit or specific error-resolution dead
