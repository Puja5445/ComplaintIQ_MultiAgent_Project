# ComplaintIQ report g3-answerable3-1794808467

Based on the evidence, the following limits apply to telephone call frequency by debt collectors:

**General Prohibition**
A debt collector must not place telephone calls or engage any person in telephone conversation repeatedly or continuously with intent to annoy, abuse, or harass any person at the called number [C:12CFR1006.14-c0].

**Presumption of Compliance (Safe Harbor)**
Subject to certain exclusions, a debt collector is presumed to **comply** with this rule if telephone calls to a particular person, in connection with the collection of a particular debt, are placed:
- No more than **seven times within seven consecutive days**; and
- **Not within seven consecutive days** after having had a telephone conversation with that person about the debt [C:12CFR1006.14-c0].

**Presumption of Violation**
The regulation also establishes a presumption that a debt collector **violates** this provision under certain conditions, though the specific triggering details are cut off in the evidence provided [C:12CFR1006.14-c0].

**Related Timing Restrictions**
Separately, a debt collector must not communicate or attempt to communicate with a consumer at any unusual time, or at a time the debt collector knows or should know is inconvenient. In the absence of the debt collector's knowledge of circumstances to the contrary, a time before 8:00 a.m. and after 9:00 p.m. local time at the consumer's location is inconvenient [C:12CFR1006.6-c0], [C:exam_manual-p668-c0].

**Note:** The evidence does not fully specify the exact conditions that trigger the "presumption of violation" standard, as that portion of [C:12CFR1006.14-c0] is truncated. Additionally, evidence does not address call-frequency limits for third-party contacts beyond the general rule that third parties may not be contacted more than once absent specific exceptions [C:exam_manual-p669-c0].
