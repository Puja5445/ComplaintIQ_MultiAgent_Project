# ComplaintIQ report g3-answerable14-1794808931

Based on the evidence, for **deposit accounts**, the controlling notice requirement is found in 12 CFR 1030.5 (Regulation DD):

**Advance notice required:** A depository institution must give advance notice to affected consumers of any change in a term required to be disclosed under § 1030.4(b) if the change may reduce the annual percentage yield or adversely affect the consumer. The notice must include the effective date of the change and must be mailed or delivered **at least 30 calendar days before the effective date of the change** [C:12CFR1030.5-c0].

**Exceptions — no notice required for:**
- Variable-rate changes (changes in interest rate and corresponding APY changes in variable-rate accounts) [C:12CFR1030.5-c0]
- Changes in check printing fees [C:12CFR1030.5-c0]
- Changes in any term for time accounts with maturities of one month or less [C:12CFR1030.5-c0]

**Separate requirement for automatically renewing time accounts:** For time accounts with a maturity longer than one month that renew automatically at maturity, institutions must provide the required disclosures before maturity, mailed or delivered at least 30 calendar days before maturity [C:12CFR1030.5-c0].

---

Note: If your question instead concerns electronic fund transfer account terms (rather than deposit account terms under Regulation DD), a different rule applies under 12 CFR 1005.8: a financial institution must mail or deliver written notice at least 21 days before the effective date of any change in a term required to be disclosed under § 1005.7(b) if the change would increase fees, increase consumer liability, reduce the types of available EFTs, or impose stricter limitations on frequency or dollar amount of transfers, subject to a security exception [C:12CFR1005.8-c0][C:exam_manual-p688-c0]. This rule is distinct from the deposit-account notice described above.
