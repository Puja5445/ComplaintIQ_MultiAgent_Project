Based on the EVIDENCE provided, the following escrow account rules apply to mortgage servicers under RESPA (12 CFR 1024.17):

## Escrow Account Analysis
Before establishing an escrow account, a servicer must conduct an analysis to determine the periodic payments and the amount to be deposited. The servicer shall use an escrow disbursement date that is on or before the deadline to avoid a penalty and may make annual lump sum payments to take advantage of a discount [C:exam_manual-p513-c0].

## Transfer of Servicing
If the new servicer changes either the monthly payment amount or the accounting method used by the old servicer, it must provide the borrower with an initial escrow account statement within 60 days of the date of transfer. The new servicer shall use the effective date of the transfer of servicing to establish the new escrow account computation year. If the new servicer retains the monthly payments and accounting method used by the old servicer, it may continue to use the same computation year established by the old servicer or choose a different one using a short year statement [C:exam_manual-p513-c0].

## Shortages, Surpluses, and Deficiencies
- If a **shortage** exists, the servicer may require the borrower to repay the shortage in equal monthly payments over at least a 12-month period [C:exam_manual-p514-c0].
- If a **deficiency** exists, the servicer may require additional monthly deposits to eliminate it:
  - If the deficiency is less than one month's escrow payment, the servicer may: allow it to exist and do nothing; require repayment within 30 days; or require repayment in two or more equal monthly payments.
  - If the deficiency is greater than or equal to one month's escrow payment, the servicer may allow it to exist and do nothing, or require repayment in two or more equal monthly payments.
  - These provisions apply as long as the borrower's mortgage payment is current at the time of the escrow account analysis [C:exam_manual-p514-c0].
- A servicer must notify the borrower at least once during the escrow account computation year if a shortage or deficiency exists [C:exam_manual-p514-c0].

## Short-Year Statements (12 CFR 1024.17(i)(4))
Short-year statements can be issued to end the escrow account computation year and establish the beginning date of the new computation year. They may be provided upon transfer of servicing and are required upon loan payoff. The statement is due to the borrower within 60 days after receiving the pay-off funds [C:exam_manual-p515-c0].

## Timely Payments (12 CFR 1024.17(k))
The servicer shall pay escrow disbursements by the disbursement date, using a date on or before the deadline to avoid a penalty, and may make annual lump sum payments to take advantage of a discount [C:exam_manual-p515-c0].

## Recordkeeping (12 CFR 1024.17(l))
Each servicer shall keep records that are easily retrievable, reflecting the servicer's handling of each borrower's escrow account. The servicer shall maintain records for each escrow account for at least five years after the servicer last serviced the account [C:exam_manual-p515-c0].

## Related Transfer Disclosures
Examiners assess whether servicers notify consumers of changes in escrow account requirements resulting from a transfer of servicing, reviewing samples of transferred loans with escrow accounts, consumer disclosures, and evidence of delivery [C:exam_manual-p133-c0].

## Broader Statutory Context
RESPA (12 U.S.C. 2601 et seq.) requires lenders, mortgage brokers, or servicers to provide borrowers with disclosures regarding settlement costs and "places limitations upon the use of escrow accounts." The National Affordable Housing Act of 1990 amended RESPA to require disclosures for mortgage escrow accounts at closing and annually thereafter, itemizing charges paid by the borrower and amounts paid out of the account by the servicer [C:exam_manual-p496-c0].
