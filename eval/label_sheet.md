## 1. After a consumer notifies a financial institution of an electronic fund transfer error, what must the institution do and within what time?

**Answer:**
## Financial Institution Obligations Upon Receiving an EFT Error Notice

### Timeliness of Consumer Notice
If a notice of error is based on documentation or clarification that the consumer requested under paragraph (a)(1)(vii), the consumer's notice is timely if received by the financial institution no later than **60 days** after the institution sends the requested information [C:12CFR1005.11-c1].

### Investigation and Resolution Timeline

**10-Business-Day Standard Period:**
- The institution must **investigate promptly** and, except as otherwise provided, determine whether an error occurred within **10 business days** of receiving the notice of error [C:12CFR1005.11-c1].
- The institution must **report results to the consumer within 3 business days** after completing the investigation [C:12CFR1005.11-c1].
- If an error is found, the institution must **correct the error within 1 business day** after determining that an error occurred [C:12CFR1005.11-c1].

**45-Day Extended Period:**
- If the institution is unable to complete its investigation within 10 business days, it may take up to **45 days** from receipt of the notice of error to investigate and determine whether an error occurred, subject to conditions specified in the regulation that are not fully set out in the available evidence [C:12CFR1005.11-c1].

**Extended Time Periods (Special Circumstances):**
The time periods above are extended as follows [C:12CFR1005.11-c2]:
- **20 business days** applies in place of 10 business days if the notice of error involves an electronic fund transfer to or from the account within **30 days after the first deposit** to the account was made.
- **90 days** applies in place of 45 days for completing the investigation if the notice of error involves an electronic fund transfer that:
  - Was not initiated within a state;
  - Resulted from a point-of-sale debit card transaction; or
  - Occurred within 30 days after the first deposit to the account was made.

### Investigation Standards
With the exception of transfers covered by § 1005.14, a financial institution's review of its own records regarding an alleged error satisfies the requirements of § 1005.11 if: (i) the alleged error concerns a transfer to or from a third party; and (ii) there is no agreement between the institution and the third party for the type of electronic fund transfer involved [C:12CFR1005.11-c2].

## 2. What counts as a billing error for an open-end credit account, and what must the creditor do on receiving notice?

**Answer:**
## What Counts as a Billing Error

Under 12 CFR 1026.13(a), a "billing error" for an open-end credit account means any of the following:

1. **Unauthorized extension of credit** — A reflection on or with a periodic statement of an extension of credit that is not made to the consumer or to a person who has actual, implied, or apparent authority to use the consumer's credit card or open-end credit plan [C:12CFR1026.13-c0].

2. **Improperly identified extension of credit** — A reflection of an extension of credit not identified in accordance with §§ 1026.7(a)(2) or (b)(2), as applicable, and 1026.8 [C:12CFR1026.13-c0].

3. **Property/services not accepted or not delivered as agreed** — A reflection of an extension of credit for property or services not accepted by the consumer or the consumer's designee, or not delivered as agreed [C:12CFR1026.13-c0].

4. **Failure to credit payment** — A reflection of the creditor's failure to properly credit a payment or other credit issued to the consumer's account [C:12CFR1026.13-c0].

5. **Accounting/computational error** — A reflection of a computational or similar error of an accounting nature made by the creditor [C:12CFR1026.13-c0].

6. **Extension of credit requiring clarification** — A reflection of an extension of credit for which the consumer requests additional clarification, including documentary evidence [C:12CFR1026.13-c0].

7. **Failure to mail/deliver statement** — A further category concerns "the creditor's failure to mail or deliver a periodic statement to the consumer's last known address" where that address was received by the creditor in writing — but the evidence provided cuts off mid-sentence at "if that address was received by the creditor, in writing, at least 20 days before the end of the billing cycle for which the statement was required," so the complete, final text of this provision (including confirmation of the "20 days" figure) is not fully verifiable from the evidence as quoted [C:12CFR1026.13-c0].

## 4. What limits apply to how often a debt collector may call a consumer?

**Answer:**
Based on the evidence, the following limits apply to telephone call frequency by debt collectors:

**General Prohibition**
A debt collector must not place telephone calls or engage any person in telephone conversation repeatedly or continuously with intent to annoy, abuse, or harass any person at the called number [C:12CFR1006.14-c0].

**Presumption of Compliance (Safe Harbor)**
Subject to certain exclusions, a debt collector is presumed to **comply** with this rule if telephone calls to a particular person, in connection with the collection of a particular debt, are placed:
- No more than **seven times within seven consecutive days**; and
- **Not within seven consecutive days** after having had a telephone conversation with that person about the debt [C:12CFR1006.14-c0].

**Presumption of Violation**
The regulation also establishes a presumption that a debt collector **violates** this provision under certain conditions, though the specific triggering details are cut off in the evidence provided [C:12CFR1006.14-c0].

## 6. When is a consumer liable for unauthorized electronic fund transfers, and how does timely reporting limit that liability?

**Answer:**
## Consumer Liability for Unauthorized Electronic Fund Transfers

### Threshold Conditions for Liability

A consumer may be held liable for an unauthorized electronic fund transfer **only if** the financial institution has provided the disclosures required by § 1005.7(b)(1), (2), and (3) [C:12CFR1005.6-c0]. Additionally, if the unauthorized transfer involved an access device, the device must have been an "accepted access device," and the institution must have provided a means to identify the consumer to whom it was issued [C:12CFR1005.6-c0].

These required disclosures include: (1) a summary of the consumer's liability under § 1005.6, (2) the telephone number and address for reporting unauthorized transfers, and (3) the institution's business days [C:12CFR1005.7-c0].

### Key Principle: Liability Based Solely on Promptness of Notice

Under the EFTA, **there is no bright-line time limit** within which consumers must report unauthorized EFTs [C:exam_manual-p697-c0]. Instead, the extent of a consumer's liability is determined **solely by the consumer's promptness in notifying the financial institution** [C:exam_manual-p697-c0]. Regulation E does not permit financial institutions to rely on consumer negligence or on a deposit agreement to impose greater consumer liability for unauthorized EFTs than is permitted under Regulation E [C:exam_manual-p727-c0].

### Tiered Liability Limits Based on Timing of Notice

**1. Notice within two business days of learning of loss/theft of access device:**
Liability is limited to the **lesser of $50 or the amount of unauthorized transfers** that occur before notice to the institution [C:12CFR1005.6-c0].

**2. Notice given after two business days ("timely notice not given"):**
Liability is limited to the **lesser of $500 or the sum of**:
- $50 or the amount of unauthorized transfers occurring within the first two business days, whichever is less; **and**
- The amount of unauthorized transfers occurring after the close of two business days and before notice to the institution [C:exam_manual-p698-c0].

**3. Notice within 60 calendar days of transmittal of the periodic statement showing the first unauthorized transfer:**
The financial institution must **not** hold the consumer liable for unauthorized transfers occurring **after** the 60-day period [C:exam_manual-p728-c0].

**4. Notice NOT given within 60 calendar days of that statement transmittal:**
The consumer's liability does **not exceed the amount of unauthorized transfers

## 8. What is a cardholder's maximum liability for unauthorized use of a credit card?

**Answer:**
A cardholder's liability for unauthorized use of a credit card "shall not exceed the lesser of $50 or the amount of money, property, labor, or services obtained by the unauthorized use before notification to the card issuer" [C:12CFR1026.12-c0].

A cardholder may be held liable for unauthorized use at all only if certain conditions are met, including that the credit card is an accepted credit card and the card issuer has provided adequate notice to the cardholder [C:12CFR1026.12-c0].

Additionally, if state law or an agreement between the cardholder and card issuer imposes a lesser liability than the $50 cap, that lesser liability governs instead [C:12CFR1026.12-c1].

## 10. What consent is needed before a bank charges overdraft fees on ATM and one-time debit card transactions?

**Answer:**
Before a bank may charge overdraft fees on ATM and one-time debit card transactions, it must obtain the consumer's **affirmative consent (opt-in)**, following these specific steps:

**1. Provide a segregated written notice**
The institution must first provide the consumer with a notice in writing, or if the consumer agrees, electronically, that is segregated from all other information and describes the institution's overdraft service [C:exam_manual-p736-c0].

**2. Provide a reasonable opportunity to opt in**
The institution must provide a reasonable opportunity for the consumer to affirmatively consent (opt in) to the overdraft service for ATM and one-time debit card transactions [C:exam_manual-p692-c0] [C:exam_manual-p736-c0].

**3. Obtain affirmative consent**
The institution must obtain the consumer's affirmative consent, or opt-in, to the institution's payment of ATM or one-time debit card transactions [C:exam_manual-p692-c0] [C:exam_manual-p736-c0]. This is described as an important feature of the opt-in process — the consumer's affirmative consent is necessary before the institution may charge overdraft fees for paying an ATM or one-time debit card transaction [C:exam_manual-p694-c0].

**Consent must be separate and distinct**
- The consent must be separate from other consents or acknowledgments (including a consent to receive disclosures electronically) [C:exam_manual-p694-c0].
- Check boxes are allowed, but the check box and the consumer's signature may only apply to the consumer's consent to opt in [C:exam_manual-p694-c0].
- Preprinted disclosures about the overdraft service provided with a signature card or contract do **not** constitute affirmative consent [C:exam_manual-p694-c0].
- Consent may be given via a website (clicking a box and a button to affirm), or in person using a form at a branch [C:exam_manual-p694-c0].

**4. Mail or deliver written confirmation**
After obtaining consent, the institution must mail or deliver written (or, if the consumer agrees, electronic) confirmation of the consent, including a statement informing the consumer of the right to revoke consent [C:exam_manual-p692-c0].

**Timing of fee assessment**
An institution complies if it adopts reasonable procedures to ensure that it assesses overdraft fees only for transactions paid *after* mailing or delivering the confirmation to the consumer [C:exam_manual-p692-c0] [C:exam_manual-p736-c0].
